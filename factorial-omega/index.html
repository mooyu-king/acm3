How to play Factorial Omega

Each day in the apartment asks you to divide your attention between income, energy, and your relationship with the android. If you work whenever the computer is available, you may have money and no energy left for the rest of the day. If you spend the day talking and resting, the next daily charge still matters. Pick one priority for the session, then make space for a second action that prepares you for tomorrow.

Use the guide below to identify the controls and choose a first route. The screenshots show menus and actions in the browser build, with the room artwork omitted so you can focus on the controls. If you have played the desktop release, treat the embedded game as a separate run with its own rules and saves. Familiar concepts can help you find your bearings, but the clock, costs, and dialogue displayed here are the ones to follow.

If you are only trying to see how the game works, take a shift and compare the counters before exploring. If you want to understand how dialogue and routine fit together, try a conversation and a meal before you finish the day.

First session checklist

Tick each step as you try it. These checkboxes are for this page and do not change your save.

▶ Go to the game

Read the apartment before taking a shift

Factorial Omega begins in a small apartment shared with an android roommate. Once the introduction ends, take a moment to find the day, time, credits, energy, and trust in the status bar. The room offers shortcuts for actions such as Work and Rest, while the action dock gives you labeled buttons for Talk, Work, Cook, Rest, and Shop. These two ways of reaching an action are useful on different screens: a mouse can target a room hotspot easily, while the dock gives a smaller touchscreen a predictable place to look.

Start by reading the current objective, then try one action and compare the status bar before and after it. Record the time alongside any change in credits or energy. When a result appears, read the message before clicking again. If a dialogue panel opens, choose a reply or close the interaction as the available controls allow. This makes it easier to tell whether you are looking at a completed action, an open choice, or an action the game declined because a requirement was missing.

A first visit is a good time to locate the door, the computer, and the bed. Think of the apartment as a map with only a few destinations, and of the dock as a shortcut list for the most common decisions. The door gives you a way to explore outside the home. If you are still learning the layout, use the labeled controls for routine actions and save exploration for a point when you know how much energy and time you can spare.

Balance credits, energy, and time

The work action turns available time and energy into credits. In this browser build, a shift takes four hours, costs 28 energy, and starts at a payout of 18 credits. Later shifts can pay more, so the first payout is a useful baseline rather than a permanent rate. If you run low on energy, another shift may be refused. Look at the energy counter before committing, especially if you want enough left for an outside trip.

Factorial Omega gives you two ways to recover energy at home. Rest gives a larger recovery and uses four hours. Cook uses two hours and requires either a noodle pack in inventory or enough credits to cover a meal. Cooking can also build trust. Use rest when you need a larger recovery and have time; consider a meal when you have supplies and would rather keep part of the day free. Both actions are easier to evaluate when you check the before and after values.

The day changes as time advances, and the current build deducts an eight credit daily cost. Plan around the next useful action and the next charge instead of spending down to the price of a single purchase. For example, after an early shift you can check whether food is already in inventory, set aside enough for the next day, and then decide whether a visit to the shop adds anything useful. Watch the time when combining work and rest late in the day.

Make dialogue choices you can compare

Talk opens a conversation with the apartment’s android resident. Read the prompt and each available answer before choosing. The build tracks trust, and a conversation may give different feedback depending on your reply or whether you already talked that day. Watch the counter and the response together. A changed counter tells you something about the result, while the actual line tells you how the character reacted.

If a reply gives an unexpected result, note the day and the action immediately before it. For a fair comparison in another session, look for the same prompt under similar conditions. A different prompt after work, a meal, or a day change may represent a different situation. This approach gives you a useful record without needing to reveal later conversations or infer a route from a single number.

A practical way to organize an evening is to do one conversation before deciding whether to cook or explore. If you have already talked, use the objective and log to pick the next action instead of assuming another click will repeat the same reward. The action dock stays useful when returning from a conversation because you can choose the next step without hunting for the roommate or a hotspot in the room.

Shop with a goal and explore when ready

The delivery terminal shows your balance and available supplies. A noodle pack costs six credits, a replacement battery costs sixteen, and a small keepsake costs twenty four in this build. Read each description before buying. A meal has an immediate use when you need to recover energy, while a spare item may be worth holding for a later interaction. The fact that you can afford an item does not tell you whether buying it now improves the rest of your day.

After a purchase, check the updated balance and the relevant inventory count. The terminal can stay open after you buy something, so look for the confirmation and close it when you have what you need. If funds are too low, work or a later outside trip gives you another way to earn credits. A declined transaction is also useful feedback: it lets you decide whether to save toward that purchase or use an action that is already available.

You can leave the apartment through the door hotspot. Outside trips consume time and energy and can return credits. A short trip can fit between other decisions, but it may leave you with less energy for work. If you have a specific goal, compare the likely benefit of exploring with what a shift or meal would accomplish. Finish the return dialogue before making a second decision so the game can record the completed action.

Save progress and resume on the same browser

Factorial Omega stores progress in browser storage on the game host. After a successful action you can check for its result, then open the game menu and choose Save progress when you want a clear checkpoint. The menu also has Resume, Fullscreen, and Main menu controls. Returning to the menu saves the current progress and gives you a way to verify that Continue is available.

When returning another day, open the same page from the browser profile you used before. Choose Continue at the game menu if you want to keep your existing run. Starting a new game creates a fresh state and can replace the current local save. If Continue is missing, check whether you opened a private window, switched profiles, cleared site data, or used a browser configuration that restricts storage in embedded frames.

Before a longer session, try a small checkpoint test: complete one action, save, return to the menu, and continue. Confirm that the day and counters match the state you just left. This takes less time than finding out at the end of a long session that your browser did not keep the save. The checklist on this page lives separately from the game, so completing a checklist item never changes the state of the apartment.

Set up the browser player for desktop or mobile

Use the Play button in the frame and confirm that you are an adult. The browser then loads the game from its dedicated host, where the player may ask for its own confirmation. On a desktop, click inside the frame to send keyboard input to the game. Check the game menu for fullscreen and use it if the room labels or smaller buttons are difficult to read.

On a phone, rotate to landscape when the game asks. Horizontal space gives the status bar, room, and action dock room to coexist. Keep an eye on the difference between a webpage gesture and a game tap. If a swipe moves the containing page, use a labeled button instead of trying to tap the same spot repeatedly. A larger display can make it easier to read dialogue choices without zooming.

If you see the game frame but cannot make progress, first note what actually loaded: an age prompt, the game menu, the room, or a browser error. Wait for the menu and try a single deliberate click. If the embedded game still fails to respond after you have checked orientation and focus, reopen the page in a regular browser window. Factorial Omega FAQ

What is Factorial Omega on this page?

It is an adults-only browser reconstruction of a dystopian apartment simulation. The current build offers dialogue, remote work, cooking, rest, shopping, outside trips, and a local save.

How do I start playing?

Choose Play in the player, confirm you are at least 18, and follow the prompts in the game frame. Then select New game or Continue.

What should I do on my first day?

Read the status bar, talk to the android, try a shift if you have enough energy, and keep enough credits for food and the daily charge.

How do I restore energy?

Use Rest for a larger recovery or Cook for a shorter action that uses food or credits.

Will my progress transfer to another device?

The current game stores its save in the browser profile on the device you use. It has no account sync or desktop save import.

Can I play on a phone?

The player provides touch controls and an orientation prompt. Use landscape for more room. Device and browser behavior can vary.

Does the player include every original system?

The embedded build covers the apartment and daily routine described here. Systems such as stock trading, streaming, and full character customization are outside this guide and current player scope.

What happens if I press New game?

New game starts a fresh run and can replace the active local save. Use Continue to return to your existing progress.
