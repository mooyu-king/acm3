Guide · 01

What the detention premise asks you to do

Problematic Subjects opens with a detention premise at the fictional Ravenscroft College. The central task is to discover what different adult characters prefer and remember how each responds. That makes the experience more than a sequence of pictures. Choices have a context, responses provide clues, and a better-informed later choice may produce a different result. The official description frames this as a quick game built around learning the cast. This guide therefore starts with a method for observing the interface and recording preferences, while avoiding a promise that every route will appear in one sitting.

Treat each new encounter as a small experiment. Read the dialogue, identify the available action, and notice the character's reaction before choosing again. If an action clearly works, remember which character received it and whether it cost a resource or advanced the current day. If it fails, preserve that information too. Repeating a failed choice without understanding the response may waste an opportunity. The point of a first session is to build a reliable mental map of the detention loop, not to chase a particular ending before you understand the rules.

Guide · 02

Map preferences without guessing from appearances

The creator says remembering each guy's preferences is central to progress. A good note has three fields: character, action, and observed reaction. Record what happened rather than what you expected to happen. Someone's clothing, manner, or first line may invite a guess, but an observed response is stronger evidence. If two actions look similar, test them in separate opportunities and compare their results. This is especially useful when a later session offers more choices than the first, or when a new skill changes the meaning of an old route.

Keep notes short enough to scan while playing. A mark such as confirmed, rejected, or untested is more useful than a long speculative paragraph. Do not assume that one person's successful action is universally good for everyone. The detention setting asks you to learn individuals, and a route guide should preserve that distinction. Our interactive checklist below is deliberately separate from game data; it can remind you to take notes, but it does not inspect your session, identify characters, or claim that an action succeeded.

Guide · 03

Use the tutorial before optimizing a run

The official listing identifies an interactive tutorial and a one-button accessibility option. Begin there, even if you have played another choice game. Tutorial prompts can reveal when an input is accepted, when a transition is still running, and which part of the screen holds the next decision. On a phone, a tap near the browser edge can be mistaken for browser navigation; confirm that the game has focus and the intended control is visibly active. On desktop, pause after each animation so that a late response does not get confused with the next click.

Plan one calm practice run before trying to maximize a score. Test the menu, locate the gallery, learn how to continue dialogue, and find the game's own save or restart controls if they exist in the edition you use. The official page lists touchscreen and mouse input, but browser behavior can vary by device and build. This site's browser reconstruction has passed desktop and simulated mobile-landscape checks, but it remains a reconstruction rather than an equivalent recovery of every original rule. These instructions stay preparatory until secure account access and real-phone testing are complete.

Guide · 04

Plan a day around resources and feedback

A choice is easier to evaluate when you know what it costs. The browser reconstruction currently under local development contains a day structure, stamina, scores, and skill choices, but it is not an exact recovery of the original Unity game. Use those concepts as a planning lens, not as proof that every edition calculates points the same way. Before selecting another action, look at the resource counters actually visible in your version. If an option is unavailable, check whether the issue is resource cost, progression, or a missing prior choice instead of clicking the disabled control repeatedly.

A useful habit is to separate observation from optimization. During the first day, learn how feedback is displayed and whether a character's response changes after a successful action. During later days, compare a similar decision under similar conditions. If the game offers upgrades, read what the upgrade says and then watch for a visible change before treating it as effective. Avoid copying a score formula from another version or a comment thread without testing it. Updates can revise balance, and a strategy that worked for one release may be a poor guide to another.

Guide · 05

Approach unlocks and the gallery deliberately

The official page advertises more than 33 adult animations, a gallery mode, two levels, and multiple endings. Those are creator claims about the listed project, not a promise that this browser reconstruction contains every original route. Gallery entries can be a useful index of what you have discovered, but a locked thumbnail alone does not explain its route. When an entry opens, note the preceding character, action, day, and any visible condition. That gives you a practical trail to reproduce it later. If a gallery hint appears, read it before searching for a spoiler-heavy solution.

Do not expect the gallery to unlock simply because you saw an image in a guide. The game may require a completed action, a threshold, or an ending state, and the exact condition can change across versions. A clean test starts from a known save or fresh run, changes one decision, then checks the gallery afterward. If nothing changes, record that outcome rather than inferring a bug. Likewise, opening a gallery item is not proof that every related variation is available. Separate what the game shows, what a guide suggests, and what you personally verified.

Guide · 06

Keep ending experiments reproducible

A route to an ending is a sequence, not a single final click. If your edition offers saves, keep a stable checkpoint before an important branch and a separate experimental checkpoint before a risky choice. If it does not, write down the key decisions as they happen. Capture only the details needed to reproduce the result: the day, visible resource state, character, selected action, and whether the result screen appeared. This method helps distinguish a genuinely different ending from a cosmetic change in a repeated scene.

When comparing endings, change one factor at a time when possible. A different score, choice, and upgrade in the same replay make it hard to identify the cause. If a route appears blocked, first check whether the current edition includes that level or ending. The official listing describes two levels, but availability on an external listing does not establish that this site's future browser build will contain both. The local browser reconstruction mentioned in the project history is incomplete and must not be described as an equivalent release until its scope and behavior are verified.

Guide · 07

Use this page on desktop and mobile

The official game listing says it can be played through a browser on a phone and identifies mouse and touchscreen input. For a future player here, the practical test will be whether the game frame fits the available screen, whether important controls remain reachable, and whether a touch actually activates the intended action. A mobile page can be responsive while its embedded game is not. Landscape orientation may offer more room for a wide game, but it should not be assumed to fix every input or memory problem. Reload only after considering whether the current progress has been saved.

Large browser games may take time to start and may react differently on a private tab, an older device, or a browser that clears storage. If the future account-gated embed seems frozen, wait for a clear loading or error state rather than tapping the same control repeatedly. When reporting an issue, include the device, browser, approximate point in the game, and the last visible action. Avoid sending passwords or private account details. The browser build is now hosted, but this page intentionally does not launch it until the required secure email access path is complete.

Guide · 08

Understand the access and age boundary

Problematic Subjects is intended for adults only. A sign-in address is an account identifier, not evidence of age. The planned player must require a separate adult confirmation and an email-authenticated session before it launches. At present, this site has Google sign-in for its existing guide account, while email-password registration remains disabled pending production email delivery, confirmation, and recovery checks. The protected browser build is hosted, but the access panel still blocks launch because the required account path is not complete.

A front-end check alone cannot protect a public game URL. The current game host restricts iframe parents and signs asset requests, but it does not yet verify a Supabase email session. The same rule applies to age and any future entitlement: do not equate a checked box or a visible account email with a secure authorization system. Before launch, this site still needs a verified email provider, tested sign-up and recovery paths, and a host-side account decision. Until then, use the guide and checklist as planning tools only.

Problematic Subjects FAQ

Can I play Problematic Subjects on this page today?+

Not yet. The protected browser build is hosted, but the required email sign-in and host-side account verification are not active. This page remains a guide preview.

Does Google sign-in unlock this game?+

No. This proposed game gate specifically requires an email-authenticated session. The site's existing Google option does not count as that method.

Is an email address proof that I am over 18?+

No. The planned adult confirmation is separate from sign-in, and neither should be presented as a formal identity or age verification service.

What should I record in a first session?+

Record the adult character, your chosen action, the visible response, and any resource or day change. Separate confirmed results from guesses.

Does the checklist sync with the game?+

No. It is a temporary page control for planning. It does not read your game state, save account data, or unlock gallery entries.

Are all levels and endings in this site's browser version?+

No equivalence claim is made. The hosted build is a browser reconstruction with a verified five-day loop and endings, while some original branches and systems remain outside its confirmed scope.

Can I use a phone when access opens?+

The browser reconstruction passed simulated mobile-landscape layout checks, but real Android and iPhone play still need separate testing before mobile support is promised.

Why is there no download or empty frame?+

The hosted game is intentionally not launched until the required email account gate and server-side authorization are complete. An empty frame would imply working access when that requirement is not ready.
